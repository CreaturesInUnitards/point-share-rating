The stat

PSR starts from the simplest thing a scoreboard can tell you: a team's share of the points scored in its games, on a 0–100 scale where 50 is league average. Every rating on the front page decomposes exactly into the layers below — point share, schedule, recency, reversion — and the pieces sum to the number shown.

The layers

1 · Smoothing. Raw point share is unstable in shutouts and low-scoring blowouts, so each game is scored as (PF + k) / (PF + PA + 2k) × 100 with k = 14 phantom points per side. A 3–0 win reads as a narrow edge, not perfection.

2 · Schedule. An SRS-style iterative opponent adjustment: AdjPSR = GamePSR + (OppRating − 50), iterated until ratings and adjustments agree. Beating good teams counts for more.

3 · Recency. Games are weighted 0.5 ^ (games_back / 12) — a twelve-game half-life. Recent form matters more than September, but no game ever fully ages out within a season.

4 · Reversion. Ratings are shrunk toward a prior of 8 phantom games at a target of 50 plus 30% of the team's prior-season deviation. Early on this keeps small samples sane; even late in the season it retains real weight, because extreme results are always part skill, part fortune — and pulling hot teams back toward average is, empirically, what predicts best.

5 · Quarterback. At prediction time only: if a team starts a QB other than its recent primary starter (the modal starter over its last eight prior games, once it has at least two), the projected margin drops by 4.21 points, a penalty fit on 2015–2021 data.

From rating to margin

Each PSR above 50 is worth about 1.71 points of scoreboard margin on a neutral field; home field adds 1.70 points. Expected home margin is 1.71 × (PSR_home − PSR_away) + 1.70, minus the QB penalty where it applies.
